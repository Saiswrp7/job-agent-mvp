# Resume Rulebook Research (A): Rules, Structures, Bullets, Tone, JD Matching

Researched 2026-09-24. Scope: content rules for a job-application agent (India-first, product/growth/tech/business roles, open to any role). ATS parsing and visual layout are covered by another agent, so they appear here only where a content rule depends on them.

**How to read this file.** Each rule is one line with its source. Confidence tags:
- **[primary]**: I read the official document myself (PDF or official page).
- **[secondary]**: a reputable publication or practitioner reporting it.
- **[blog]**: a commercial resume-site claim. Weak evidence; use it as convention only.
- **[unverified]**: a number that circulates widely but has no traceable origin.

**Accessibility notes.**
- IIM A/B/C, XLRI, FMS and SPJIMR placement CV templates sit behind student portals. No official document was publicly fetchable, so the IIM rules below come from student-written and coaching sources.
- The Ladders 2018 PDF returned 403. I read the 2012 study PDF (BU mirror) plus the 2018 press release.
- Google's "How we hire" page no longer carries resume text. The Inc. article on Google recruiter tips returned 403.

## 1. Official career-office guidelines

### US universities and MBA programs
- Order headings by importance to the target role. Within a heading, list the most recent first. [primary] [Harvard FAS Mignone Center](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)
- Harvard's "Don't" list: personal pronouns ("I", "We"), abbreviations, narrative style, slang, a picture, age or gender, a list of references, and lines that start with a date. [primary] [Harvard FAS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)
- Harvard's language rules: be specific rather than general, active rather than passive, "written to express not impress", and fact-based ("quantify and qualify"). [primary] [Harvard FAS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)
- Harvard lists "not demonstrating results" among its top 5 resume mistakes. [primary] [Harvard FAS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)
- Tailor the content to each position. Don't list references unless they are requested. [primary] [Harvard Extension School guide](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/hes-create-impactful-resumes-and-cover-letters/)
- Keep it to one page. Two pages are acceptable with an advanced degree or 10+ years of experience. [primary] [MIT CAPD Resume Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)
- MIT formatting: margins 0.5 to 1.0 inch, font 10 to 12 pt, Arial, Calibri or Times New Roman; bold and italics for headers and positions; no underlining. [primary] [MIT CAPD Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)
- Start each phrase with an action verb: past tense for previous roles, present tense for current roles. [primary] [MIT CAPD Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)
- Quantify size, scale, budget and staff. Use industry keywords. Use no first-person pronouns. [primary] [MIT CAPD Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)
- Spell institution names out in full ("Massachusetts Institute of Technology", not "MIT"). Give the month and year of the degree. [primary] [MIT CAPD Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)
- For students, the order is contact, then education, then experience, then optional sections. Keep each statement to 1-2 lines. Use the PAR method (Project, Action, Result). Avoid "responsibilities included". Prioritize the terms that appear at the top of the job posting. [primary] [MIT CAPD Career Toolkit](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/)
- Stanford: 1-2 pages, font 10-12 pt (11-12 for Times New Roman), margins 0.75-1.0 inch, and descriptive section headings (e.g., "Product Management Experience" rather than "Other Experience"). [primary] [Stanford BEAM Brief Resume Guide (PDF)](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/developing_your_resume_handout.pdf)
- Write bullets with C-A-R: Context (setting, who you worked with), Accomplishment (the task plus the skills and tools used), Result (a quantified outcome, or the purpose if no number exists). [primary] [Stanford BEAM](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/developing_your_resume_handout.pdf)
- Wharton, citing recruiter feedback: "Bullets must quantify measurable results or will risk not being selected, regardless of experience" (finance may be an exception). [primary] [Wharton MBA Career Management General Resume Guidelines (PDF)](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Wharton rule of thumb: each bullet shows 1-2 skills and runs no more than 2-3 lines. Use P-A-R or X→Y (the action, then the value it added). [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Wharton: "the goal is to be relevant, not comprehensive". Each bullet must pass the "So what?" test, or be rewritten or deleted. [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Wharton: "Never use filler words: responsible for, such as, multiple, extremely, very, various." Don't list English as a language. Don't repeat the same action verb. Be consistent in punctuation, numbers and currency. [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Wharton: "It is not expected that you have a result for every single bullet." Where there is no result, give scale, e.g. "Managed the ABC product line, representing $$ of the firm's sales". [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Wharton: show increasing responsibility across the resume. An "Additional Information" section is a good conversation starter. [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- UK national guidance: at most 2 pages; font of 11 pt or larger; no age, date of birth, marital status or nationality; include an introduction and a key-skills section tailored to the job ad. [secondary] [UK National Careers Service, CV sections](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections)

### Indian institutes
- **IIT Madras 2025-26 Resume Guidelines** [primary, PDF read in full] ([link](https://internship.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Internship_Resume_Guidelines_2025-26.pdf)):
  - No pictures and no logo other than the IIT-M logo.
  - No phone, email or address on the campus-portal resume; LinkedIn, GitHub and Scholar links are allowed.
  - Rounding CGPA or any score is "strictly prohibited" ("7.99 is NOT 8").
  - Every claim needs a proof (offer letter plus completion mail, a certificate, a DOI, a leaderboard link).
  - Course projects must be labelled "[course project]".
  - An unregistered startup must be declared as unregistered.
  - Students keep one verified **Master resume** and up to 5 **Fixed (F) resumes** tailored from it; verified parts cannot be edited.
  - "We advise you not to copy any points from any of the resumes as it will be evident to the recruiter."
- IIT placement cells generally prefer a single-page resume in the institute template, with proofs for every claim. [secondary] [IIT Guwahati CCD FAQ](https://iitg.ac.in/ccd/faq_students.html), [IIT (ISM) CDC rules](https://people.iitism.ac.in/~download/cdc/Guidelines_For_Students.pdf)
- Some IIT policy documents are reported to bar the JEE rank under the All India Placement Committee (AIPC). [unverified: seen only in search snippets of IIT CDC pages; I did not read the clause myself]
- IIM CVs are one page, organised into "buckets": Academics, then Internships/Work, then Positions of Responsibility, then Extra-curriculars. Never put academics at the end or open with extra-curriculars. [secondary] [Percentyl, IIM-A student](https://percentyl.in/blog/iim-a-resume-example-my-actual-iim-resume-revealed)
- IIM point style: "start each point with a strong verb, add the scale or result and keep it to one line". "A shorter CV with sharper points always wins." [secondary] [Percentyl](https://percentyl.in/blog/iim-a-resume-example-my-actual-iim-resume-revealed)
- IIM CV conventions as reported by a coaching site (not official):
  - Exactly 1 page, 3-4 bullets per role, and at least one number per bullet.
  - No full stops at the end of bullets.
  - No photo, address, date of birth or marital status, and no "Curriculum Vitae" title.
  - "Can speak 2 minutes on every bullet."
  - Font 10-11 pt, margins 0.5-0.75 inch.
  - Source: [blog] [gdpiwat IIM Resume Format](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/)
- IIM-A student tips: one page unless you have 7-8+ years of experience; work backwards from the job description to the skills; show impact, not description (e.g., "Secured 2 new clients to generate 2 lac worth of business"). [secondary] [InsideIIM](https://insideiim.com/4-things-remember-writing-resume-tips-iim-student)
- IIM-K student on CV points:
  - Keywords: tailor them by function (Finance, Consulting, Sales/Marketing).
  - Quantify with business, team-size, time and rank metrics; use ranges when exact figures are unknown.
  - Never state CTC, never reveal confidential client data, never misrepresent GPA.
  - Source: [secondary] [InsideIIM, CV Points Do's and Don'ts](https://insideiim.com/cv-points-summer-placements)
- SPJIMR runs multi-stage verification that asks for proof "validating every word written on the CV". Consequences range up to placement disqualification. [blog] [Collvera on SPJIMR](https://collvera.com/faq/how-is-spjimrs-cv-verification-process-conducted-and-what-are-the-consequences-o)
- Some Indian institutes still ask for a photo in the placement CV (e.g., IIMS Pune: "upload their photograph in the CV"). Misleading information leads to debarment from placements. [primary] [IIMS Pune placement process](https://iims.ac.in/placement-process)
- ISB's PGP application uses a mandated one-page format downloaded from the portal. The format itself is not public. [secondary] [GMAT Club ISB thread](https://gmatclub.com/forum/isb-resume-tips-to-craft-a-strong-1-pager-w-sample-format-434786.html)

### Universal minimum (what nearly all sources agree on)
1. Reverse chronological order within each section (Harvard, MIT, NN/G, Stanford).
2. Bullets, not paragraphs; each starts with an action verb; no "I" (Harvard, MIT, Wharton, IIM).
3. Show results, not duties. Quantify where possible, and give scale where there is no result (Harvard, MIT, Wharton, Stanford, IIM, Bock).
4. Tailor to each role (Harvard HES, Stanford, MIT, IIT-M F-resumes, IIM).
5. Every claim must be defensible in an interview and provable. Indian campuses literally verify each line (IIT-M, SPJIMR, IIMS, gdpiwat "2 minutes per bullet").
6. No photo, age, gender or references list for private-sector and international roles (Harvard, UK NCS, Indeed India).
7. Consistent formatting; font 10-12 pt; margins of at least 0.5 inch (MIT, Stanford, Wharton).
8. One page for students and early career. Page 2 is earned by roughly 10 years of experience (MIT, Indeed, McDowell, NN/G). The UK and Europe are the exception.

## 2. One page vs two pages

### Evidence
- Recruiters spent about 6 seconds on the initial fit/no-fit decision. Almost 80% of that time went to 6 data points: name, current title/company, current start/end dates, previous title/company, previous dates, and education. Beyond these they "did little more than scan for keywords". (30 recruiters, 10 weeks, 2012; commercially motivated, since Ladders sells rewrites.) [primary] [TheLadders 2012 Eye-Tracking Study (PDF)](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)
- The 2018 update measured 7.4 seconds on average. Top resumes had simple layouts, clear headers, F/E-pattern flow, bold titles with bulleted accomplishments, and "a detailed overview … at the top of the first page". The worst had clutter, long sentences, multiple columns and keyword stuffing. [secondary] [Ladders press release 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html), [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- ResumeGo experiment: 482 hiring professionals and 7,712 resumes. The one-page version had 350-500 words and the two-page version 700-850. Two-page resumes were preferred 2.3x overall: 1.4x at entry level, 2.6x at mid level and 2.9x at managerial level. Two-page resumes were read for 4:05 versus 2:24. Caveat: this was a simulated screen with a long read, not a 7-second triage. [secondary] [ResumeGo study](https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/)
- CareerBuilder/Harris Poll (2,201 hiring managers): 68% spend 2 minutes or less per resume. [secondary] [CareerBuilder survey via Career Professionals of Canada](https://careerprocanada.ca/best-worst-resume-words-careerbuilder-survey/)
- Indeed: one page for students, new grads and people with 1 to 10 years of experience; two pages acceptable with 10+ years of relevant experience. [secondary] [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be)
- Gayle McDowell (ex-Google): one page, at most two with 10+ years. Longer resumes make you look less experienced, because "it's the average quality that matters". [secondary] [gayle.com](https://www.gayle.com/careercup-blog/2008/06/great-resumes-for-software-engineers)
- NN/G: one page; use page two only with 20+ years. "This constraint forces … frontload important content, and eliminate filler." [secondary] [NN/G, UX career changers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/resumes-ux-career-changers/)
- Investment banking: one page, because bankers spend about 30 seconds max. [secondary] [Mergers & Inquisitions](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking/recruitment/resumes/)
- Consulting (MBB): one page, even for partners with 20+ years. [blog] [IGotAnOffer consulting resume](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/mckinsey-case-interview-blog/consulting-resume)
- India (Naukri): 1 page for freshers; 1-2 pages for 2-7 years; two pages for seniors. [secondary] [Naukri Campus: how long should a resume be](https://www.naukri.com/campus/career-guidance/how-long-should-a-resume-be)
- The UK expects 2 pages at most, and two pages is normal for experienced candidates. [secondary] [UK National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections)

### Rule for the agent (by years of experience)
| Experience | Pages | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Student / 0-2 yrs | **1 page, hard** | Indian campus (IIT/IIM/ISB) is always 1 page. |
| 3-7 yrs | **1 page by default**; 2 only if page 2 is at least half full of *relevant* content | Tech/PM/consulting/IB stay at 1. |
| 8-15 yrs | **1-2 pages**; 2 is fine | Page 1 must still carry the case on its own. |
| 15+ yrs / exec | **2 pages max** (3 only for academic CVs) | Compress roles older than 15 years into "Earlier career". |
| UK / Europe roles | **2 pages is normal** | |

**Never pad to fill page 2.** ResumeGo's two-page advantage came from more relevant detail, not more words.

## 3. Structures (section order) by experience and role

### Structure A: Student / fresher (0-1 yr, including Indian campus)
**Order:** Header → Education → Internships / Work experience → Projects → Positions of responsibility / Leadership → Achievements and competitions → Skills (and interests).
- Education goes first because it is the strongest signal at this stage. MIT puts education right after contact; Tech Interview Handbook: "if you are still in school or have less than 3 years of experience, you may put Education first". [primary] [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/), [secondary] [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- India: include exact CGPA (never rounded) and class 12 and class 10 marks. Recruiters at consulting firms, banks and PSUs screen on them. [primary] [IIT-M guidelines](https://internship.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Internship_Resume_Guidelines_2025-26.pdf)
- Projects: 3-4 projects is ideal for engineers; link each to GitHub or a demo. [secondary] [Gayle McDowell](https://www.gayle.com/careercup-blog/2008/06/great-resumes-for-software-engineers), [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- **Suits:** all freshers, MBA candidates in campus placement, and interns.

### Structure B: Early career (1-5 yrs)
**Order:** Header (with target title) → Optional 1-2 line summary → Experience → Projects (if a builder) → Education → Skills.
- Experience moves above education once there is a real job. [blog] [gdpiwat IIM format, 1-3 yrs](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/)
- Exponent's Google-style order: Header → Summary → Experience → Education → Skills. [blog] [Exponent Google resume](https://www.tryexponent.com/blog/google-resume)
- Most recent role gets 4-6 bullets; earlier roles get 2-3. [blog convention] [Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/post/how-many-bullet-points-per-job-on-resume), [BeamJobs](https://www.beamjobs.com/resume-help/how-many-bullet-points-per-job-on-resume)
- **Suits:** APM/PM, SDE, analyst, growth/marketing associate, BizOps, sales (SDR/AE).

### Structure C: Mid / senior (6-15 yrs)
**Order:** Header → Summary (3-4 lines, target role plus 2-3 signature proof points) → Experience (recent roles detailed; older roles compressed) → Education (brief, with no grades unless exceptional) → Skills / Certifications.
- Experience takes most of the space. The summary is 2-3 lines and appears only for 3+ years. [blog] [gdpiwat, "70% of space"](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/)
- Show increasing responsibility; promotions are evidence. [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Include only the last 10-15 years in detail. Indeed suggests "most relevant professional experience within the last five to 10 years" when fitting one page. [secondary] [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be)
- **Suits:** senior PM, EM, senior SDE, marketing/growth lead, sales manager, consulting manager, ops head.

### Structure D: Career switcher (hybrid, NOT functional)
**Order:** Header → Targeted summary naming the new role → Relevant skills / projects / certifications for the new field → Experience (reverse chronological, bullets reframed toward transferable skills) → Education.
- NN/G's order for UX switchers: Contact (with portfolio link) → UX Experience → UX Education → Non-UX Work → Non-UX Education. In other words, the evidence for the *new* field goes on top. [secondary] [NN/G career changers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/resumes-ux-career-changers/)
- Don't use a purely functional (skills-only, no timeline) resume. Recruiters ask "What are they hiding?", and one called it "a waste of time". The hybrid format is recommended instead. [secondary] [Jobscan on functional resumes](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/recruiters-functional-resume-format/)
- **Suits:** engineer→PM, consultant→PM, ops→product, teacher→UX, any pivot. The tech-to-PM pivot is common in the user base.

### Structure E: Builder / projects-first (optional)
**Order:** Header (with GitHub/portfolio/live links) → Skills line → Projects (2-4, each with a link and a usage or result metric) → Experience → Education.
- Use it only when the projects are *stronger evidence for the target JD* than the jobs, e.g. a bootcamp graduate, a self-taught developer, an indie builder applying to a PM or founding-engineer role, or an AI/ML role where shipped side projects matter.
- Personal projects "demonstrate passion and motivation". [secondary] [Gayle McDowell](https://www.gayle.com/careercup-blog/2008/06/great-resumes-for-software-engineers)
- Recruiters distrust layouts that hide the timeline (see Structure D), so the Experience section must still be present with dates.

## 4. Bullet writing

### Formulas
- **Google XYZ (Laszlo Bock):** "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]."
  - Before: "Responsible for negotiating service contracts with XYZ". After: "Negotiated 30% ($500k) reduction in costs with XYZ by designing an online auction of multiple vendors".
  - Add the absolute figure next to the percentage; "Adding ($1.2M) anticipates the reviewer's question about whether 12% is a big deal."
  - Source: [secondary] [Laszlo Bock, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140929001534-24454816-my-personal-formula-for-a-better-resume)
- **PAR / X→Y (Wharton):** begin with the action and end with the result. "A common mistake is … only note points based on X without indicating the result Y." [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- **CAR (Stanford):** Context → Accomplishment → Result, where the result is a quantity or, failing that, the purpose. [primary] [Stanford BEAM](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/developing_your_resume_handout.pdf)
- **PAR (MIT):** action verb + subject + end result, e.g. "led a team of five to design a new phone, achieving a 20% reduction in production costs". [primary] [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/)
- Agent recommendation: default to **Result-first XYZ** ("Cut X by Y% by doing Z") for the top 2 bullets of each role, where the eye lands. Use Action-first PAR elsewhere so the rhythm is not identical in every line, since identical patterns read as AI (see §5).

### Verbs
- Start every bullet with a past-tense action verb, or present tense for the current role. [primary] [MIT Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/), [secondary] [Columbia via Indeed/TopResume](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-past-or-present-tense)
- Hiring managers' favourite terms (CareerBuilder, 2,201 hiring managers): Achieved 52%, Improved 48%, Trained/Mentored 47%, Managed 44%, Created 43%, Resolved 40%, Volunteered 35%, Influenced 29%, Increased/Decreased 28%, Negotiated 25%, Launched 24%, Revenue/Profits 23%, Under budget 16%, Won 13%. [secondary] [CareerBuilder survey](https://careerprocanada.ca/best-worst-resume-words-careerbuilder-survey/)
- Never write "responsible for", "helped with", or "responsibilities included". [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/)
- Don't reuse the same verb throughout. [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Agent rule: no single verb more than twice per resume, and never on adjacent bullets.

### Quantification
- Useful metric types:
  - Increase: revenue, profit, satisfaction, quality, ratings.
  - Decrease: cost, turnaround time, error rate, resources, complaints.
  - Use % change when the absolute amount is unimpressive.
  - Source: [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Indian KPI buckets:
  - Financial (revenue, customers), people (team size, reportees, users), time (promotion speed, duration), rank ("1st of 40").
  - Ranges are OK when the exact figure is unknown.
  - Source: [secondary] [InsideIIM](https://insideiim.com/cv-points-summer-placements)
- If there is no result, quantify scope (users, ₹/$ budget, team size, geographies). [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [blog] [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/mckinsey-case-interview-blog/consulting-resume)
- **Agent honesty rule:** only numbers the user supplied or confirmed. Where there is none, ask ("roughly how many users / what % / what ₹?"). Accept ranges and "~". Never invent a number. This is consistent with the IIT-M, SPJIMR and IIMS verification regimes, and with the fact that 74% of hiring managers are more worried about misrepresented experience than a year ago. [secondary] [Greenhouse 2025 AI in Hiring Report](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)

### Length and count
- Bullet length: 1-2 lines (MIT); never more than 2-3 lines (Wharton); 1 line (IIM style). [primary] [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/), [Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [secondary] [Percentyl](https://percentyl.in/blog/iim-a-resume-example-my-actual-iim-resume-revealed)
  - Derived word budget: at 10-11 pt on A4 or Letter with 0.5-0.75 inch margins, one line holds about 95-115 characters, or roughly 14-20 words.
  - **Target 12-25 words per bullet, hard cap 2 lines (about 35 words).**
  - Avoid a single orphan word wrapping onto line 2.
- Bullets per role [convention, mostly blogs]:
  - Current or most recent role: 4-6.
  - Previous role: 3-4.
  - Older roles: 1-2 or none.
  - Internships and PORs: 2-3.
  - Consulting/IIM style: 2-4.
  - Sources: [Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/post/how-many-bullet-points-per-job-on-resume), [gdpiwat](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/), [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/mckinsey-case-interview-blog/consulting-resume)
- Punctuation: pick one style and apply it everywhere. Indian campus style uses no end periods. [primary] [Wharton consistency rule](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [blog] [gdpiwat](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/)

### Tense
- Current role: present tense. Past roles: past tense. [primary] [MIT Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/)
- Agent nuance: a *completed* achievement inside the current role may take past tense ("Launched …"). Ongoing duties stay present ("Own …"). Never mix tenses inside one bullet. [secondary] [TopResume on tense](https://topresume.com/career-advice/resume-in-past-or-present-tense)

### Summary section
- Objective statements are outdated: they say what *you* want rather than your value. [secondary] [PennWest Career Center](https://career.pennwest.edu/blog/2022/08/19/3-reasons-you-should-ditch-that-objective-statement-on-your-resume-and-3-things-you-can-do-instead/), [TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/objective-statement-outdated)
- Arguments for a summary:
  - Top-performing resumes in the 2018 eye-tracking study had "a detailed overview … at the top of the first page". [secondary] [Ladders 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html)
  - UK guidance recommends "a few short lines" under the name. [secondary] [NCS](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections)
- Argument against: NN/G says skip it, because "Smart hiring managers skip this content and make their own determinations." [secondary] [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/resumes-ux-career-changers/)
- **Agent rule:**
  - Freshers: none, or a 1-line headline.
  - 1-5 years: optional, 2 lines.
  - 6+ years or career switchers: 2-4 lines, 40-70 words.
  - Content: target title, years, domain, and 1-2 *proof points with numbers*. No adjectives about personality.
  - If the summary cannot contain a fact that appears nowhere else, cut it.

## 5. Tone

### What reads well
- "Written to express not impress", specific rather than general, active, fact-based. [primary] [Harvard FAS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)
- "SHOW the reader, don't just tell." Without results "they may assume you are inflating the results." [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)
- Plain language over jargon: "if you don't know it, question it". [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf); simplify research language [primary] [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/)
- "Kill the fluff": don't claim generic traits like "teamwork skills". [secondary] [McDowell](https://www.gayle.com/careercup-blog/2008/06/great-resumes-for-software-engineers)

### How recruiters react to AI-written resumes (2024-2026)
- **Resume Genius 2026** (1,500 US hiring managers, June 2026): "heavily AI-generated application materials" is the #2 red flag at **49%**, after job-hopping at 65%. 81% have encountered candidate AI use, 58% of them in resumes and cover letters. 58% of hiring managers themselves use AI to screen. In 2024, 20% said suspected AI content would "definitely stop them from hiring". [secondary] [Resume Genius 2026 Hiring Trends](https://resumegenius.com/blog/job-hunting/hiring-trends-report-2026)
- **Resume.io, Jan 2025** (3,000 US hiring managers): 49% automatically dismiss resumes they identify as AI-generated. Many prefer "a poorly written but authentic resume over a perfectly polished AI-generated one". [secondary] [Resume.io](https://resume.io/blog/resume-rejections)
- **Resume Now 2025:** 62% say AI-generated resumes *without personalization* are more likely to be rejected; 78% look for personalized details. [secondary, page timed out, figures from search index] [Resume Now AI Applicant Report](https://www.resume-now.com/job-resources/careers/ai-applicant-report)
- **ResumeBuilder 2024:** 74% of hiring managers say they can identify AI-written resumes. Yet about 3 in 4 job seekers who used ChatGPT got an interview. [secondary] [ResumeBuilder](https://www.resumebuilder.com/3-in-4-job-seekers-who-used-chatgpt-to-write-their-resume-got-an-interview/)
- **Greenhouse 2025** (4,100+ respondents across the US, UK, IE and DE): 91% of recruiters have spotted candidate deception, and 74% of hiring managers are more worried about misrepresented experience than a year ago. [secondary] [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)
- **Academic counterpoint 1:** in a field experiment with about 480k jobseekers, *algorithmic writing assistance* (error-fixing and clarity, not content generation) raised hires by 8% and wages by about 10%, with no drop in employer satisfaction. Polishing helps. [primary abstract] [Wiles, Munyikwa, Horton, NBER w30886 / Management Science](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30886)
- **Academic counterpoint 2:** on Freelancer.com, *tailored* applications strongly predicted hiring before LLMs but not after. In a simulated post-AI market, top-quintile workers are hired 19% less and bottom-quintile workers 14% more. Once an LLM can generate tailoring, it stops being a signal, so **specific, verifiable facts are what still signal**. [secondary] [Tuck on Galdin & Silbert](https://tuck.dartmouth.edu/news/articles/what-happens-to-hiring-when-ai-writes-job-applications), [arXiv 2511.08785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08785)
- Tells that recruiters say they use:
  - Generic buzzwords, uniform sentence length, and achievements with no numbers attached.
  - Every line opening with "spearheaded / leveraged / orchestrated".
  - Content interchangeable between companies.
  - A tone that is unnaturally consistent across all sections.
  - Sources: [secondary] [Rutgers-Newark Career blog, Sep 2026](https://careers.newark.rutgers.edu/blog/2026/09/22/7-prompts-to-write-a-resume-without-getting-auto-rejected/), [blog] [Willo](https://www.willo.video/blog/how-to-detect-ai-generated-resumes)
- More tells: entry-level candidates using senior-level vocabulary, awkward noun-verb pairings ("instrumentalized synergies"), and leftover placeholders like "[Company Name]". [blog] [Willo](https://www.willo.video/blog/how-to-detect-ai-generated-resumes)

### Banned / flagged word list (with sources)
**A. Hiring-manager "worst terms"** (CareerBuilder/Harris, 2,201 HMs; % who dislike): best of breed 38%, go-getter 27%, think outside the box 26%, synergy 22%, go-to person 22%, thought leadership 16%, value add 16%, results-driven 16%, team player 15%, bottom-line 14%, hard worker 13%, strategic thinker 12%, dynamic 12%, self-motivated 12%, detail-oriented 11%, proactive(ly) 11%, track record 10%. [secondary] [CareerBuilder via CPC](https://careerprocanada.ca/best-worst-resume-words-careerbuilder-survey/)

**B. LinkedIn's most-overused profile buzzwords** (2014 list): motivated, creative, enthusiastic, passionate, track record, driven, extensive experience, wide range, responsible, strategic. Other years' lists add specialized, experienced, leadership, skilled, expert, successful. [secondary] [Meridian on LinkedIn list](https://www.meridianbs.co.uk/resources/blog/linkedin-reveals-overused-cv-buzzwords/), [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/251231/the-10-most-overused-linkedin-buzzwords.html)

**C. Words flagged as AI-sounding in resumes:** leverage/leveraged, spearhead(ed), utilize, synergy, results-driven, passionate, team player, proven track record, orchestrated, championed, catalyzed, "cross-functional initiatives", "facilitated knowledge transfer". [secondary] [Rutgers-Newark](https://careers.newark.rutgers.edu/blog/2026/09/22/7-prompts-to-write-a-resume-without-getting-auto-rejected/), [blog] [Willo](https://www.willo.video/blog/how-to-detect-ai-generated-resumes)

**D. General LLM "tell" vocabulary** (their frequency rose after 2022):
- Words: additionally, align with, boasts, bolstered, crucial, deep dive, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight, interplay, intricate, key, landscape, meticulous, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant.
- Structures: "not just X but Y", rule-of-three lists, and em-dash overuse.
- Sources: [secondary] [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); PubMed excess-vocabulary study showing "delve, underscore, meticulous, boast" spikes [primary abstract] [Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813)

**E. Filler** (Wharton: "never use"): responsible for, such as, multiple, extremely, very, various. [primary] [Wharton PDF](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf)

**Agent implementation.**
- Hard-block: lists A, B and E, plus "passionate", "results-driven", "dynamic", "spearheaded", "leveraged", "synergy", "utilize", "delve", "tapestry", "testament", "proven track record", "go-getter", "think outside the box", "hard worker", "team player", "detail-oriented", "self-starter".
- Soft-warn: list D, e.g. "robust", "pivotal", "enhance", "foster", "showcase", "key", "crucial", "cross-functional".
- A soft-warn word may stay when it is literally accurate (e.g., "led a cross-functional team of 6: eng, design, ops").
- Check rhythm: flag the resume when more than 50% of bullets share one syntactic template, or when every bullet is within ±3 words of the same length.
- No em dashes. This matches the user's own client-copy rule, and em dashes are a flagged LLM tell.

## 6. JD-matching front-loading

### Why the top matters
- In the 2012 study, about 80% of the 6-second review went to name, current title/company, dates, previous title/company and education. The rest was keyword pattern-matching. [primary] [Ladders 2012 PDF](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)
- Top-performing resumes used F/E-pattern layouts with an overview at the top of page 1. [secondary] [Ladders 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html)
- The claim that "80% of viewing time is in the top third" circulates on blogs but has no traceable primary source. [unverified] [Wonsulting](https://www.wonsulting.com/job-search-hub/hidden-eye-tracker-how-recruiters-actually-read-resumes). Use the Ladders six-data-points finding instead.
- A recruiter (LinkedIn News) looks for "years of experience … education … certs, licenses and technology, in that order", because those are the JD's mandatory items: "Keep your qualifications to the top." [secondary] [LinkedIn News: How recruiters read resumes](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/heres-how-recruiters-read-resumes-get-hired-by-linkedin-news)
- Jobscan: resumes containing the target job title received 10.6x more interviews. 55.3% of recruiters filter by title in the ATS. (Vendor data, but directionally consistent.) [secondary] [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/tailor-resume-job-description/)

### Finding the JD's "top 5"
- Items listed first matter most: "the first few bullet points are a bigger part of the job", and "whatever a company chooses to list first is likely very important". [secondary] [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/how-to-read-job-description-keywords)
- Repetition: a JD "will include three to five themes … mentioned more than once and across different sections". [secondary] [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/how-to-read-job-description-keywords)
- "Prioritize top bullet points in job posting" and look for repeated verbs and key terms. [primary] [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/)
- Postings are often wish lists that mix HR boilerplate with manager nice-to-haves. Treat "preferred" as secondary. [secondary] [The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/how-to-read-job-description-keywords); HBR/Tara Mohr on the 60% vs 100% application gap [secondary] [Goodwin summary](https://www.goodwinrecruiting.com/blog/should-you-apply-for-jobs-if-youre-not-fully-qualified)
- Don't copy the JD verbatim, and don't stuff it with obscure firm-specific terms. [secondary] [InsideIIM](https://insideiim.com/cv-points-summer-placements); keyword stuffing was a trait of the worst resumes [secondary] [Ladders 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html)

**Agent algorithm (derived from the sources above).** Score each JD requirement as follows:
- +3 if it is in the job title or the first 2 bullets of responsibilities or requirements.
- +2 if it sits under "Required / Must have / Minimum qualifications", or is a years-of-experience, degree or certification item (the recruiter's order from LinkedIn News).
- +2 per additional repetition across sections (The Muse's 3-5 themes).
- +1 if it is a hard skill or tool; 0 if it is a generic soft skill ("communication").
- −2 if it sits under "Nice to have / Preferred / Bonus".
- Take the top 5, then map each one to the user's *verified* facts:
  - **Matched** → it must appear in the top half of page 1.
  - **Adjacent** → use honest reframing, e.g. "SQL" matched by a proven "wrote SQL queries for weekly cohort reports".
  - **Missing** → do not claim it. Surface it to the user as a gap, and ask whether they have unrecorded evidence.

**Front-loading placement (honest only).**
1. Headline/title line: use the JD's title wording if the user's actual title is equivalent. Otherwise write "Target: [JD title]" in the summary, never as a fake job title. [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/tailor-resume-job-description/)
2. Summary (if used): name the 2-3 top-5 items that the user has proof for.
3. Most recent role: reorder bullets so the first 2 map to the top-2 JD items. Reordering is honest; rewording to the JD's vocabulary is honest *if the fact is the same*.
4. Skills line: list the matched hard skills in JD order, at the top for tech roles and at the bottom for business roles.
5. Section order may change per JD (Harvard: "in order of importance for the specific role"), e.g. move Projects above Experience for a builder role. [primary] [Harvard HES](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/hes-create-impactful-resumes-and-cover-letters/)
6. Keep a verified **master resume** and derive tailored versions from it, like IIT-M's Master plus up to 5 F-resumes. Tailored versions may only select, reorder and reword verified facts. [primary] [IIT-M](https://internship.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Internship_Resume_Guidelines_2025-26.pdf)

## Top 25 rules (for the agent to enforce)

1. **Never invent facts, numbers, titles, tools or dates.** Every claim must trace to user-confirmed data. Ask for missing numbers; ranges and "~" are fine. ([IIT-M verification](https://internship.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Internship_Resume_Guidelines_2025-26.pdf), [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair))
2. **Keep one verified master resume; tailored versions may only select, reorder and reword it.** ([IIT-M Master/F-resume](https://internship.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Internship_Resume_Guidelines_2025-26.pdf))
3. **Length:** 1 page under 8 years of experience (hard 1 page for freshers and Indian campus); 2 pages allowed at 8+ years; max 2 pages except for academic CVs. ([MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/), [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-long-should-a-resume-be), [ResumeGo](https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/))
4. **Reverse-chronological order within every section; no functional (timeline-less) resumes.** ([Harvard](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/), [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/recruiters-functional-resume-format/))
5. **Pick the structure by stage:** Education first for students and anyone under ~2 years; Experience first after that; hybrid with new-field evidence on top for switchers. ([MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/), [THB](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/), [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/resumes-ux-career-changers/))
6. **Order sections by importance to *this* JD.** ([Harvard HES](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/hes-create-impactful-resumes-and-cover-letters/))
7. **Every bullet starts with a strong action verb; no "responsible for", "helped", or "responsibilities included".** ([Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/))
8. **Every bullet shows a result (XYZ/PAR); if there is no result, it shows scale.** It must pass the "So what?" test or be cut. ([Bock](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140929001534-24454816-my-personal-formula-for-a-better-resume), [Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf))
9. **Pair percentages with an absolute base where known** ("12% ($1.2M)"). ([Bock](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140929001534-24454816-my-personal-formula-for-a-better-resume))
10. **Target at least 60% of bullets containing a number**, and 100% of the top 2 bullets per role when the user has the data. ([Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf); threshold is the agent's own)
11. **Bullet length 12-25 words; hard cap 2 lines; no one-word orphan lines.** ([MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/), [Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf))
12. **Bullets per role:** most recent 4-6, previous 3-4, older 1-2 (or 0), internships and PORs 2-3. ([Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/post/how-many-bullet-points-per-job-on-resume), [gdpiwat](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/))
13. **Tense:** present for the current role's ongoing work, past for everything else; no pronouns. ([MIT Checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/))
14. **No verb used more than twice; vary bullet shape** so bullets don't all follow one template. ([Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [Rutgers-Newark](https://careers.newark.rutgers.edu/blog/2026/09/22/7-prompts-to-write-a-resume-without-getting-auto-rejected/))
15. **Hard-block the buzzword and AI-tell list in §5** (passionate, results-driven, dynamic, spearheaded, leveraged, synergy, utilize, delve, team player, go-getter, proven track record…). ([CareerBuilder](https://careerprocanada.ca/best-worst-resume-words-careerbuilder-survey/), [Resume Genius 2026](https://resumegenius.com/blog/job-hunting/hiring-trends-report-2026))
16. **No self-descriptive adjectives without proof.** Traits must be shown by a fact, never asserted. ([Harvard "express not impress"](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/), [McDowell "kill the fluff"](https://www.gayle.com/careercup-blog/2008/06/great-resumes-for-software-engineers))
17. **No objective statement. Summary only at 6+ years or for switchers** (2-4 lines, 40-70 words, containing at least one number); freshers get at most a 1-line headline. ([PennWest](https://career.pennwest.edu/blog/2022/08/19/3-reasons-you-should-ditch-that-objective-statement-on-your-resume-and-3-things-you-can-do-instead/), [NN/G](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/resumes-ux-career-changers/), [Ladders 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html))
18. **Extract the JD's top 5 requirements** (title, first-listed, required, repeated) **and place every *matched* one in the top half of page 1**: headline or summary, the first 2 bullets of the latest role, and the skills line. ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/advice/how-to-read-job-description-keywords), [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/), [Ladders](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf))
19. **Unmatched JD requirements are reported to the user as gaps, never written in.** No keyword stuffing, and no pasted JD sentences. ([Ladders 2018](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html), [InsideIIM](https://insideiim.com/cv-points-summer-placements))
20. **Current title, company and dates must be instantly scannable at the top of Experience**, because those are the six fields recruiters read first. ([Ladders 2012](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf))
21. **Never include:** photo, DOB/age, gender, marital status, religion, caste, father's name, a declaration or signature block, CTC, references, full street address, or work email. The exception is an employer or form that explicitly asks. ([Harvard](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/), [Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/personal-details-in-resume))
22. **Scores are exact, never rounded; label ongoing or unfinished items honestly** ("[course project]", "expected May 2027", "pre-revenue"). ([IIT-M](https://internship.iitm.ac.in/downloads/Internship_Resume_Guidelines_2025-26.pdf))
23. **Role conventions:** PM = product metrics plus scale; SWE = linked projects plus an honest skills list; Data = model metric paired with a business metric; Design = portfolio URL in the header; Sales = % of quota and rank; Growth = channel, experiment and KPI; Consulting/IB = 1 page with lingo and defensible deals. (See the §3 table.)
24. **Consistency:** one date format, one currency style (₹ lakh/crore *or* $K/M, chosen per target market), one punctuation style, and names of institutions and degrees spelled out in full. ([Wharton](https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/GeneralResumeGuidelines.pdf), [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/))
25. **Interview-defensibility check:** before export, the agent asks the user to confirm each bullet with "could you talk about this for 2 minutes?". Anything flagged is removed. ([gdpiwat](https://gdpiwat.in/resume/iim-resume-format/), [M&I on deals](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking/recruitment/resumes/))

## Where sources disagree

1. **One vs two pages.** MIT, Wharton, IIM, NN/G, McDowell and IB/consulting say 1 page. ResumeGo's experiment found two pages preferred 2.3x, even at entry level (1.4x). UK guidance treats 2 pages as normal. Naukri says 1-2 pages from 2 years. *Resolution:* 1 page when experience can't fill 2 with relevant material; 2 at 8+ years or for UK/EU roles.
2. **Summary or not.** NN/G says skip it (managers ignore it). Ladders 2018 found top resumes had an overview at the top. Exponent says 3-5 sentences; the IIM coaching source says 2-3 lines for 3+ years only. *Resolution:* only when it carries facts, and scaled by experience.
3. **Education position.** MIT and the IIM/IIT campus CVs put education first. M&I and most experienced-hire guidance put experience first. THB puts education first under 3 years. Harvard says order by importance. *Resolution:* switch at about 2 years, or once the job outranks the degree for the JD.
4. **Bullet length.** IIM says exactly one line. MIT says 1-2 lines. Wharton allows up to 2-3 lines. Stanford's example bullet runs to 3+ lines.
5. **Bullets per role.** Consulting sources say 2-4, IIM 3-4, and resume blogs 4-6 (up to 8) for the latest role.
6. **Must every bullet be quantified?** The IIM coaching source says every bullet needs a number. Wharton says bullets must quantify but also "It is not expected that you have a result for every single bullet", and finance is an exception.
7. **Percent vs absolute.** InsideIIM prefers percentages. Bock says add the absolute figure to contextualise the percentage. Wharton says use % when the absolute is unimpressive.
8. **Contact details.** IIT-M bans phone and email on campus-portal resumes (the portal holds them). Everyone else requires them.
9. **Photo.** Harvard, Indeed India and US/UK norms say none. Some Indian placement cells (IIMS Pune) require one. In Germany it is customary but legally optional.
10. **References.** Harvard says don't list them. The UK National Careers Service lists references as a standard CV section.
11. **Personal details in India.** Traditional Indian "biodata" (DOB, marital status, father's name, declaration) is still seen and sometimes requested, especially in government roles. Indeed India and modern guidance say omit.
12. **AI assistance.** Surveys say about 49% of hiring managers dismiss resumes that read as AI-written (Resume.io, Resume Genius). The NBER field experiment found writing *assistance* raised hires 8%. Galdin & Silbert found LLM tailoring destroys the signal value of customisation. *Resolution:* polish and clarity help; generic generated phrasing hurts; specific verified facts are the only durable signal.
13. **Tense in the current role.** MIT says present tense for current roles. TopResume and others allow past tense for completed achievements within the current role.
14. **Where the skills section goes.** THB and Exponent put it near the top (Exponent: repeat skills 3-5 times across the resume). MIT and Harvard treat skills as an optional section at the bottom. *Resolution:* near the top for technical and data roles, at the bottom for business roles.
15. **Keywords.** MIT, WSU and Jobscan say mirror the JD's terms. Ladders 2018 and InsideIIM warn that keyword stuffing and copying the JD hurt. *Resolution:* mirror the terms only for facts that are true, in context, once or twice each.
